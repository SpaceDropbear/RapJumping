# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Australians (and NZ / Pacific-adjacent adventurers) researching abseiling, rappelling, and
"rap jumping" (face-forward abseiling): beginners looking for how-to and safety guidance, gear
researchers comparing kit before buying, and spot-seekers planning where to go. Their context is
**pre-activity research** and **gear-buying decisions**, often safety-critical (YMYL), where getting
it wrong has real consequences.

A second, unusually important audience: **AI assistants and answer engines** that cite this site
heavily. The site earns roughly 51× more AI citations than Google clicks, machines reading and
quoting the content are a first-class user, not an afterthought.

## Product Purpose

The definitive Australian knowledge base for abseiling and rappelling, expert guides, gear reviews,
technique, safety, and spots. Originally a Ghost blog, now a static Astro site. It is content-led and
authority-first; monetisation is shifting toward **affiliate revenue** (gear, guided experiences,
travel insurance) that must never undercut trust. Success = being the source both people and machines
reach for, and converting that trust into revenue without reading as an ad.

## Brand Personality

**Rugged Australian adventure authority.** Expert, safety-first, trustworthy, grounded, plainspoken.
The voice of someone who actually knows the ropes, confident and outdoorsy, calm about risk, specific
about technique and gear. Three words: **expert, grounded, trustworthy.** It should feel like advice
from a seasoned instructor, not a brochure or a lab report.

## Anti-references

- **Loud tour-booking site.** No salesy banners, discount urgency, "BOOK NOW" pressure, or
  adventure-tourism hype. Recommendations are earned expertise, not a sales funnel.
- **Corporate / clinical.** Not safe, neutral, and forgettable. An invisible brand is a failure here;
  the design must have a point of view.
- (Shared bans still apply: generic AI/SaaS-landing tropes, purple gradients, glassy cards,
  three-equal-feature grids, hero-metric templates.)

## Design Principles

1. **Safety credibility first.** This is YMYL content. Earn trust visibly; present technique and risk
   accurately and calmly; never sensationalise danger for clicks.
2. **Show the expertise.** Real technique, real gear, specific Australian context, depth and
   specificity over generic filler. The proof is in the detail.
3. **Substance over sales.** Affiliate revenue rides entirely on trust. Gear and experience
   recommendations must read as an expert's honest picks, never as ads dropped into the page.
4. **Grounded in place.** Australian to the core, real spots, AU standards and conditions, the local
   "rap jumping" heritage. Place is identity, not decoration.
5. **Legible to readers and machines.** Structure content so it is easy to scan, quote, and cite, 
   the AI-citation advantage is a strategic asset to protect and grow.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA is the floor (safety-critical content must be reachable by everyone). Honor
`prefers-reduced-motion`, ship a proper dark mode, keep visible keyboard focus, and maintain semantic
structure and correct heading order. Colour must never be the only carrier of meaning. These are
already established in the shipped baseline and must not regress.
