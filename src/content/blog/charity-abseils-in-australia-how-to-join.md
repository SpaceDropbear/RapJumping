## Choose where to abseil

One of the first things you'll want to figure out is where exactly you'll be taking that big step off the ledge. You'll discover a mind-boggling variety of abseil venues in Australia:

1. City skyscrapers: Picture the Perth skyline behind you while you lower yourself down Central Park Tower. In fact, the iconic Central Park Plunge in Perth has a massive goal of raising $350,000 for local charities in 2025, including organisations like Anglicare WA and Parkerville Children and Youth Care. Over the last 10 years, it's already raked in a whopping $4.2 million.
2. Stadium abseils: How about stepping off the side of a sports stadium? In Townsville, the Abseil and Adventure Day at Queensland Country Bank Stadium attracted participants from the North Queensland Toyota Cowboys women's team. That event specifically raised funds for Brighter Lives, supporting local public health services.
3. Coastal cliffs: If city skyscrapers aren't your style, you can head to rocky coastal edges. Some events, such as the Dirk Hartog adventure, offer epic ocean views. You won't just be fundraising; you'll also be exploring Australia's rugged beauty.
4. Historical or unusual sites: Want something extra unique? You'll find abseils in disused lift shafts, waterfalls, and even down big cranes. If you're feeling especially adventurous, keep an eye out for events that highlight distinctive Aussie landscapes for a memorable experience.

There's also the QV1 Abseil in Perth, a 40-story high descent that has raised thousands of dollars for various causes. If that height makes your knees wobbly just thinking about it, remember that experienced instructors and solid safety gear will be there to guide you. ## Understand fundraising rules

Before you strap on a harness, it's a good idea to understand the basics of how fundraising works Down Under. Australia has multiple layers of regulations, and each state or territory may have its own set of fundraising laws. While that might sound complicated, you basically need to ensure your event has approval where it's actively promoted or taking place. If you're hosting a large online campaign, then it's worth confirming licenses across different regions, because each part of the country can have slightly different guidelines.

In February 2023, Australian governments agreed on a set of nationally consistent fundraising principles. The idea is to streamline rules and encourage ethical practices, but not all states and territories have adopted the new principles in full. Regardless, it helps to keep these points in mind:

- You might need an Authority to Fundraise if you're raising money for a particular charity. This applies to organisations like the Prostate Cancer Foundation Australia (PCFA), where you're required to register and follow the guidelines set by the charity before you start any big campaign.
- Different places have different rules for raffles, collection tins, and public solicitation. If you plan to shake a tin while balancing at the top of a bridge, double-check your local laws first.
- Any misleading statements to the public about how funds are used can breach Australian Consumer Law, which is enforced by the Australian Competition and Consumer Commission. It's important to be transparent about your fundraising goals, costs, and how donations will be spent.

When in doubt, hop onto the relevant state or territory regulator's website, or reach out directly to your chosen charity for clarity. A little research up front can save you a heap of trouble later.

## Secure your charity partner

A successful abseil fundraiser often hinges on the cause behind it. You'll find a wide range of charities out there. Some organisations, like Breakthrough T1D (formerly JDRF), dedicate their fundraising efforts to medical research. Others, such as Variety WA, focus on supporting children who are sick, disadvantaged, or living with a disability. If you participate in the Central Park Plunge, for instance, you can choose among charities like the Fiona Wood Foundation or genU, which provides disability support services nationwide.

### When picking your charity, you'll want to look for:

- A mission you care about deeply: If you're passionate about tackling a particular disease or supporting children, your enthusiasm will shine through and help you rally more donations.
- A track record of using funds wisely: You can usually find annual reports on charity websites to see how money is spent.
- Fundraising support: Many organisations provide marketing toolkits, guidelines, and personal fundraising pages to help you reach your goal faster.

If you plan to organise your own abseiling event, you'll need to get in touch with the charity well in advance. Most large charities are quite familiar with event fundraisers, so they'll let you know about any necessary documents. Make sure you coordinate the date, location, insurance, and collection processes. Getting it all in writing makes everything clearer for everyone involved.

## Prepare for the challenge

You may wonder: is abseiling scary? A bit, yes, but it's also thrilling, and with the right preparations, you'll feel safe every step of the way. Here's what to consider before you show up on abseil day:

- Physical readiness: Abseiling uses your core and leg muscles to control your descent. If you can, build some light fitness into your routine beforehand. Simple exercises like squats and planks can help you feel more confident.
- Gear: Organizers typically provide harnesses, helmets, and ropes. You might want to wear sturdy shoes with good grip and comfy clothes that let you move freely.
- Mental prep: It's perfectly normal to have butterflies when stepping backward over that edge. Remind yourself why you're doing this event and the difference your fundraising makes. A short pep talk can do wonders for your nerves.
- Weather considerations: Outdoor abseils might be delayed or rescheduled if conditions are unsafe. Keep an eye on communications from the organizers and factor any last-minute changes into your plans.

Some events offer practice sessions to get you comfortable before the main descent. If you feel extra nervous, ask the instructors to walk you through the entire process in detail. They usually have a wealth of tips to help beginners. And if you happen to be collecting donations on-site, consider having a friend or volunteer available to manage that so you can focus on the abseil itself.

## Raise vital funds

Raising money through an abseil isn't just about the adrenaline rush. It's also a powerful way to give back to your community or support a global cause. In many charity abseil events across Australia, a single day of dangling can generate anywhere from £4,000 to £40,000. A few examples:

- The QV1 Abseil event in Perth raised $6,000 in a single day back in April 2019, bringing participants together from all walks of life.
- Abseil for Youth in Sydney has collected over $2.6 million since 2010, helping young Australians struggling with addiction and mental health challenges.
- A group of determined dads once biked between temples in Japan and raised nearly $100,000 for childhood brain cancer research, demonstrating that adventure-based fundraising can have an enormous impact.

Most participants set an individual goal. You might start with a modest target of a few hundred dollars. Or perhaps you'll aim to surpass a friend who completed the same event last year. Whatever number you pick, share your progress widely on social media or in email updates. People love tracking your journey, especially if you include photos. If you're open to stepping even further outside your comfort zone, try a costumed abseil. When you post a snapshot of yourself rappelling off a building dressed as your favourite superhero, you'll drum up plenty of curiosity and maybe extra donations.

### Below are a few quick tips to maximise your fundraising:

- Create clear sponsorship tiers: Promise different shout-outs, logos on your abseil gear, or special social media mentions based on donation amounts.
- Stay connected: It's good etiquette to send a short thank you and recap after the event so supporters see how their contributions helped.

### FAQs

**Q. What's the quickest way to take part?**  
A. Join an existing charity abseil run by a reputable operator. They handle site, safety, insurance, and the charity portal-you focus on fundraising and the fun.

**Q. How do I run my own charity abseil?**  
A. Partner with a qualified abseil operator and a registered charity, secure venue permission, confirm insurance, get an Authority to Fundraise (where required), and publish a clear event page with costs, targets, and safety info.

**Q. What permissions or “paperwork” do I need?**  
A. Typically: venue approval, charity Authority to Fundraise (as applicable), risk assessment/SWMS, insurance certificates, and participant waivers. Keep fundraising comms transparent about where the money goes.

**Q. What gear is provided and what do I bring?**  
A. Operators provide technical kit (ropes, anchors, devices, helmets, harnesses). You bring closed shoes, comfy activewear, water, sun protection-and use an autoblock/prusik below the device on rappel.

**Q. How much should I aim to raise?**  
A. Pick a simple, motivating target (e.g., $500-$2,000 per person, or a team total). Offer sponsor tiers and ask employers about donation matching to boost totals.

**Q. Is it beginner-friendly?**  
A. Yes-guided charity abseils are designed for first-timers. You'll get a safety briefing, practice lower, and a supervised main descent.

**Q. What about kids or accessibility?**  
A. Many events allow teens with guardian consent; minimums depend on harness/device fit. Ask about adaptive harnessing and extra staff support for inclusive participation.

**Q. How do we keep it safe?**  
A. Qualified guides, redundant/equalised anchors (angles ≤60°), knots in rope ends, clear comms, edge protection, weather/heat policy, and a documented rescue plan. Participants descend one at a time with a ground belayer/fireman's backup where appropriate.

**Q. Any quick fundraising tips?**  
A. Tell a personal story, launch with your own seed donation, use matched gifts, post short updates with photos, thank donors promptly, and celebrate milestones (e.g., costume rappel at 75% of target).

**Q. What if weather turns bad?**  
A. Have a go/no-go window and a contingency date. Communicate changes early via email/SMS and your event page.
