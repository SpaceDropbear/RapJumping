## TL;DR

- **Coasteering (exploring a coastline by a mix of scrambling, swimming, climbing and cliff jumping) is a genuinely niche activity in Australia**, unlike in the UK where it originated and is far more widespread.

- **Margaret River, in Western Australia's South West, is the country's primary coasteering destination**, with a coastline of white sandy beaches, towering granite sea cliffs, limestone reefs and sea caves well suited to the activity.

- **Smaller-scale coasteering also exists on the NSW North Coast near Byron Bay** and on Tasmania's Tasman Peninsula, though options are limited compared to Margaret River.

- **Coasteering demands genuine swimming fitness and ocean-water confidence**: you are moving through and along active surf and swell, not a calm swimming pool, so it is generally run as a guided activity with wetsuits and buoyancy aids.

Coasteering is one of the more distinctive entries in Australia's adventure-activity landscape, not because it lacks spectacle, but because it is genuinely rare here. The activity originated in the UK, particularly Wales, where a dramatic, wave-battered coastline and a strong outdoor-recreation culture made it a natural fit. Australia's equivalent coastline exists, but the activity itself has only taken root in a handful of specific locations.

This guide covers what coasteering actually involves and where you can genuinely try it in Australia today.

If it is the height and adrenaline of coasteering that appeals rather than the ocean specifically, a guided [rap jumping](/) session offers a comparable hit: real exposure and height, fully supervised, without needing coastal access at all.

## What coasteering actually involves

Coasteering combines several disciplines into a single, continuous coastal journey: scrambling over rocks and along cliff edges, swimming through open water and sea caves, climbing short sections of rock, and jumping (from modest, guide-assessed heights) into deep water. Unlike canyoning, there is no fixed route or rope system involved in most coasteering. It is a dynamic, water-based activity that responds to swell, tide and weather on the day, which is exactly why it is almost always run as a guided experience with wetsuits, helmets and buoyancy aids supplied.

## Margaret River: Australia's primary coasteering destination

Margaret River, in Western Australia's South West region, is by a clear margin Australia's main coasteering destination. Its coastline offers a genuinely diverse mix of terrain well suited to the activity: white sandy beaches giving way to towering granite sea cliffs, limestone reefs channelling through to spectacular bays, and dramatic rock stacks and sea caves along the way. It is widely described as the first, and effectively the only established, home of dedicated coasteering in the country, which reflects both the coastline's suitability and the specific operator investment that has built up there over time.

## Smaller pockets: NSW North Coast and Tasmania

Outside Margaret River, coasteering exists in Australia on a much smaller scale. The NSW North Coast, a short drive south of Byron Bay on the country's most easterly coastline, offers coasteering adventures along a similarly dramatic stretch of coast. Tasmania's Tasman Peninsula, within Tasman National Park south-east of Hobart, also offers coasteering opportunities, taking advantage of the peninsula's sea cliffs and rugged coastal terrain. Both are worth knowing about if you are travelling in those regions, though options and operator availability are considerably more limited than at Margaret River.

## Who coasteering suits, and how to prepare

Coasteering demands genuine swimming fitness and comfort in open, moving ocean water. This is not a calm-pool activity, and conditions can include swell, current and cold water depending on location and season. Most operators run coasteering as a guided experience with wetsuits, helmets and buoyancy aids supplied, and assess conditions and jump heights on the day based on swell and tide, which is exactly the right approach for an activity this weather-dependent. If you are not a confident open-water swimmer, coasteering probably is not the right entry point into adventure activities, and a land-based option like via ferrata or a guided abseiling session is a better starting place. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is coasteering?

Coasteering is the exploration of a coastline using a mix of scrambling, swimming, climbing and cliff jumping, typically run as a guided activity with wetsuits and buoyancy aids supplied, since conditions depend on swell, tide and weather on the day.

### Where can I go coasteering in Australia?

Margaret River, in Western Australia's South West, is Australia's primary coasteering destination. Smaller-scale coasteering also exists on the NSW North Coast near Byron Bay and on Tasmania's Tasman Peninsula.

### Is coasteering safe?

Coasteering carries genuine ocean-based risk (swell, current and cold water), which is why it is almost always run as a guided activity with safety equipment supplied and conditions assessed by the guide on the day. It is not recommended as a self-guided activity for inexperienced ocean swimmers.

### Do I need to be a strong swimmer to try coasteering?

Yes. Coasteering demands genuine swimming fitness and comfort in open, moving ocean water, since much of the activity involves swimming between sections of scrambling and jumping.

### Why is coasteering not more widespread in Australia?

It is a niche activity here compared to its UK origins, where a dramatic coastline and strong outdoor-recreation culture made it a natural fit. Australia's coastline suits it in specific locations like Margaret River, but the activity has not developed the same widespread operator network as in the UK.

### What should I bring for a coasteering trip?

Most operators supply wetsuits, helmets and buoyancy aids as part of a guided trip, so check exactly what is included when you book. Bring sturdy shoes suitable for wet rock, sun protection and a towel and dry clothes for afterwards.
