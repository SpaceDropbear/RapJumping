Drive west out of Melbourne on a clear day and the You Yangs announce themselves long before you arrive: a cluster of granite peaks rising abruptly out of the flat Werribee lava plains, visible from a genuinely long way off. That dramatic, isolated profile is exactly what makes the You Yangs such a distinctive climbing destination: short granite tors rather than one big cliff, all within easy reach of the city.

You Yangs Regional Park sits roughly 55km south-west of Melbourne and about 22km north of Geelong, managed by Parks Victoria, and its combination of granite quality and genuine accessibility has made it a longstanding favourite for Melbourne climbers who want a real outdoor session without a long drive.

Climbing at short, exposed granite tors like these shares its foundations with [rap jumping](/) and abseiling: sound anchors, careful rope-work and a calm head at height. If the You Yangs appeal but you're new to rope systems, a guided rap jumping session is a controlled, well-supervised way to get comfortable with exposure before your first outdoor climbing session.

## About the You Yangs: granite tors on the Werribee plains

The You Yangs' defining geological feature is exactly what you notice on the drive in: a cluster of granite peaks rising abruptly out of the surrounding Werribee lava plains, visible from a long way off in every direction. That isolation is part of the park's character: there's no gradual build-up of foothills, just flat plains giving way suddenly to granite.

Managed by Parks Victoria, the park's climbing terrain is spread across a number of short granite tors rather than concentrated on one large cliff face, which suits a session-based approach to climbing: moving between different tors and problems over the course of a day, rather than committing to a single long route.

## Climbing styles: trad, sport and bouldering

The You Yangs offer a genuine mix of climbing styles across its granite tors: some trad climbing using natural gear placements, some sport climbing on bolted lines, and bouldering: rope-free climbing on shorter granite features, protected by crash pads rather than a rope system. That mix makes the park a good fit for climbers who want to sample different styles in a single visit, and it's part of why the You Yangs have a reputation as one of the better all-round granite destinations close to Melbourne.

As with other Victorian granite and trad destinations in this series, we haven't listed individual route or problem names in this article. Check a current, area-specific climbing or bouldering guide when planning an actual session, and confirm any access conditions with Parks Victoria beforehand.

## Getting there and park hours

You Yangs Regional Park is around 40 minutes' drive from Melbourne, which is a big part of its appeal as a genuinely easy outdoor climbing option rather than a full day-trip commitment. It's also well placed for Geelong-based climbers, sitting roughly 22km north of the city.

Standard park opening hours are 7am to 5pm, extending to 7am-6pm during daylight saving, with some exceptions around major holidays. Treat these hours as indicative rather than fixed. Always check current Parks Victoria opening hours before you visit, particularly if you're planning an early start or a session that runs close to closing time.

## A nearby second option: Brisbane Ranges National Park

If you're building a bigger climbing day west of Melbourne, it's worth knowing that Brisbane Ranges National Park, near Anakie, is another destination Parks Victoria lists for climbing and abseiling. We haven't gone deep on Brisbane Ranges in this article (it deserves its own dedicated look in a future guide), but it's a genuine, park-authority-recognised option in the same general area as the You Yangs, and worth a mention if you're scouting more than one crag for a west-of-Melbourne trip.

## Gear and safety at the You Yangs

Because the You Yangs mix trad, sport and bouldering, the gear you need depends on what you're planning to do: a trad rack and quickdraws for roped climbing, or just climbing shoes, a chalk bag and crash pads for bouldering. Whichever style you're climbing, granite tors like these still carry genuine fall risk: bouldering falls in particular are often underestimated by newer climbers, since there's no rope to catch you, only pads and a spotter.

As with any climbing hardware used in Australia, look for recognised certification: carabiners to EN 12275 (and/or the equivalent UIAA standard), harnesses to EN 12277, dynamic ropes to EN 892, and helmets to EN 12492. Our [Rock Climbing in Australia: The Complete Guide](/rock-climbing-in-australia-the-complete-guide/) covers these standards, and general gear and safety fundamentals, in more depth. If you're new to any of these disciplines, seek instruction or climb with an experienced partner rather than working it out solo on the rock.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far are the You Yangs from Melbourne?

You Yangs Regional Park is around 40 minutes' drive from Melbourne, roughly 55km south-west of the city, and about 22km north of Geelong: one of the more accessible outdoor climbing options for Melbourne-based climbers.

### What kind of climbing can you do at the You Yangs?

The You Yangs offer a mix of trad climbing, some sport climbing and bouldering, spread across a number of short granite tors rather than one large cliff. This makes it a good spot to sample different climbing styles in a single visit.

### What are the opening hours for You Yangs Regional Park?

Standard hours are 7am to 5pm, extending to 7am-6pm during daylight saving, with some exceptions around major holidays. Always check current Parks Victoria opening hours before you visit, as these can change.

### Is there another climbing area near the You Yangs?

Yes. Brisbane Ranges National Park, near Anakie, is a nearby second option that Parks Victoria also lists for climbing and abseiling, worth considering if you're building a bigger day out west of Melbourne.

### Do I need a rope to climb at the You Yangs?

Not necessarily. Bouldering at the You Yangs is rope-free, using crash pads and a spotter instead, while trad and sport routes on the granite tors require a rope, harness and the relevant protection gear.

### Is the You Yangs suitable for beginners?

The mix of styles and generally short, manageable granite tors make it accessible for newer climbers, but genuine fall risk still applies, especially in bouldering. Beginners should climb with an experienced partner or seek instruction rather than working it out alone.
