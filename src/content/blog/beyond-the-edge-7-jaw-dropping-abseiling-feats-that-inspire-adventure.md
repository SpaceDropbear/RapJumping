For those with a true love of **adventure** and an insatiable craving for **adrenaline, abseiling** (or **rappelling** as it's often known globally) offers one of the most exhilarating ways to interact with gravity. From the sheer faces of monolithic cliffs and the misty spray of breathtaking waterfalls, to the dizzying heights of the tallest buildings in the urban jungle, **extreme sports** enthusiasts are always looking for ways to push their limits and conquer new vertical challenges.

It's a discipline that demands precision, courage, and a unique connection to your environment. While many thrill-seekers might begin their journey with a controlled descent from a purpose-built structure, some extraordinary individuals have taken **rappelling** to an entirely different level, forging their names in the annals of incredible human daring.

Here are seven truly awe-inspiring abseiling feats that showcase the boundless spirit of human courage and ingenuity - achievements that the vast majority of us may never emulate, but can certainly admire and draw inspiration from!

## #1 Defying Age: Doris Long's 101-Year-Old Descent

Imagine taking up **abseiling** at the age of 85. Now, envision breaking your own world record by **rappelling** down the side of one of Britain's most iconic structures at the remarkable age of 101! That's precisely what the indomitable Doris Long [accomplished in July 2015](https://www.standard.co.uk/news/uk/fearless-101yearold-woman-abseils-down-tower-to-set-new-world-record-10383734.html).

"Daring Doris," as she was affectionately known, successfully descended the 560-foot (170-metre) **Spinnaker Tower** in Portsmouth. Despite battling challenging conditions like wind and rain on the day, she calmly and confidently made it safely to the ground. She consistently claimed she wasn't afraid of heights, instead relishing the unique challenge that **abseiling** presented. Her fearless spirit and dedication to raising funds for charity remain a powerful and enduring inspiration, proving that age is truly just a number when it comes to **adventure**.

What's your excuse for not seeking out your next personal challenge?

## #2 Conquering El Capitan: A Terrifying SRT Descent

**El Capitan**, a colossal granite monolith rising 3,000 feet (915 metres) from the valley floor in **Yosemite National Park**, USA, is legendary in the climbing world. While often ascended, rappelling its entire face is a feat of immense scale and technical skill.

In 2013, a group of daredevils embarked on a terrifying descent of El Capitan using what is known in the **rappel** world as the "Single Rope Technique" (SRT) for a continuous descent. This highly advanced method requires meticulous rigging and a deep understanding of rope dynamics to manage the immense length of rope over such a vast vertical distance. The descent, which began from a high rock overhang and concluded on the peaceful valley floor, is often compared to the challenge of climbing Mount Everest due to its sheer scale and the precision required. Indeed, fewer people have completed a continuous SRT descent of El Capitan than the total number of individuals who reach the summit of Everest each year, highlighting its unique and formidable nature. This **abseiling** feat truly embodies the spirit of **extreme sports**.

## #3 Maletsunyane Falls: The World's Highest Commercial Abseil

While not a specific one-off record, **Maletsunyane Falls** in Lesotho, Southern Africa, offers an **abseiling opportunity** that is itself a world record. Recognised by Guinness World Records as the [highest commercially operated **abseil**](https://www.semonkonglodge.com/world-record-waterfall-abseil-at-maletsunyane-falls/)in the world, this is a true bucket-list item for any **adrenaline** junkie.

The falls, the highest single-drop falls in Southern Africa, plunge a dizzying 669 feet (204 metres). For those without prior **abseiling** experience, a comprehensive two-day training program is required to prepare for the monumental descent. The **rappel** itself typically takes around 40 minutes, a significant duration that allows participants to truly immerse themselves in the experience. The reward? Some of the most stunning, mist-shrouded views the region has to offer, a unique perspective on nature's raw power. On average, only about four people complete this challenging descent each day, making it an exclusive and truly memorable adventure.

## #4 The Vertical Sprint: Steve Truglia's 8.99 Second 100m Abseil

Hollywood stuntman and ultimate daredevil Steve Truglia once held a [world record](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/fastest-100-m-abseil) that defied belief: the fastest 100-metre **abseil** descent. In September 2004, Truglia raced down the side of London's Centre Point tower, reaching the ground in an astonishing 8.99 seconds.

To put that into perspective, this vertical sprint was faster than Usain Bolt's 100-metre sprint world record of 9.58 seconds - a truly mind-boggling comparison for a feat involving a controlled descent! While experts from organisations like ITS Tactical (an authority on tactical skills) have pointed out some interesting safety considerations visible in footage of his extreme feat, such as the use of a single carabiner, it undeniably showcased Truglia's unparalleled skill and willingness to push the boundaries of what's possible in **rappelling**. He was, after all, a genuine daredevil who lived life on the edge.

## #5 Mascot Madness: Rappelling in Bulk

Just when you thought it was challenging enough to **abseil** or **rappel** down the side of a building in standard adventure gear, imagine doing it while wearing a bulky, oversized mascot costume! That's exactly what two beloved sports mascots from Philadelphia managed to do.

The Philadelphia sports mascots **rappelled** down the side of a 418-foot (127-metre) skyscraper to raise money for charity. This truly eye-catching feat captivated plenty of attention - after all, how often do you get to see colourful, furry characters descending the side of a towering city building? It was a unique blend of athleticism, showmanship, and philanthropy, proving that **adventure** comes in many forms, and sometimes the most outlandish challenges create the biggest impact. This **abseiling feat** brought smiles and raised significant funds, demonstrating the lighter side of **extreme sports**.

## #6 Mass Descent: 42 People Rappel Katika Waterfall, India

In 2013, a remarkable group of 42 individuals from the Youth Hostels Association (YHA) of India [set a world record](https://www.deccanchronicle.com/131216/news-current-affairs/article/andhra-pradesh-rappelling-effort-makes-it-world-record) by **rappelling** down the side of **Katika waterfall** near the famous Borra caves in Andhra Pradesh. This impressive collective **abseiling feat** involved descending a 450-foot (137-metre) cascade, setting a world record for the most people to **rappel** down a waterfall of that size.

Led by the then 64-year-old Sreenivasa Prasad, the large team completed the descent with the aim of promoting **adventure activities** amongst young people visiting the region. It was a testament to teamwork, careful planning, and a shared passion for **extreme sports**. This large-scale **rappelling** event not only achieved a world record but also inspired a new generation to embrace the thrills of outdoor adventure, demonstrating the power of a collective pursuit of **adrenaline**.

## #7 Publicity Stunt: Bear Grylls in Shanghai

In September 2015, reality TV star and survival expert Bear Grylls showcased his signature daring by **abseiling** down the side of one of **Shanghai's tallest buildings**. This high-profile stunt was a dramatic prelude to the launch of his [own brand of survival television](https://beargryllssurvivalacademy.com/) in China.

The building, which served as the base for Shanghai Media Group, presented a formidable vertical challenge. Grylls, known for his extraordinary feats of survival, made the descent with his usual unflappable demeanour, seemingly barely raising a sweat. The publicity stunt, which closely resembled a scene ripped straight from a Mission Impossible film, certainly didn't go unnoticed. It drew immense attention from both passers-by on the bustling streets below and prominent Chinese media outlets, solidifying his image as the ultimate **adrenaline** junkie and master of **extreme sports**.
