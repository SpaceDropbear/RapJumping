If via ferrata and canyoning sound like more commitment than you're after, high ropes courses and adventure parks are Australia's most accessible way into aerial adventure. They combine built, guided or self-guided infrastructure with ziplines, suspension bridges, cargo nets and platform-to-platform challenges, and they suit a much wider range of ages and fitness levels than the more technical activities covered elsewhere in this series.

This guide covers what to expect, some of Australia's best-known destinations, and the safety standards this kind of infrastructure is built to.

If you want a taste of real rope-based exposure alongside a high ropes day, a guided [rap jumping](/) session pairs well as a complementary activity: genuine height and a fully supervised abseiling introduction.

## Illawarra Fly Treetop Adventures

Illawarra Fly Treetop Adventures, in the Illawarra rainforest near Jamberoo south of Sydney, is one of Australia's best-known high ropes and treetop destinations, and is officially recognised as home to the highest zipline in the country. The site features elevated cable spans and suspension bridges linking tree platforms roughly 35 metres above the forest floor, alongside the separate Treetop Walk: a 1.5km loop with a 500m elevated steel walkway sitting 20-30m up, and the Knights Tower, a spiral lookout tower reaching around 45 metres. It's a genuinely eco-focused site set within protected rainforest, making it as much a nature experience as an adventure-activity one.

## Multi-park operators and treetop courses nationwide

Beyond dedicated destinations like Illawarra Fly, multi-park operators run self-guided treetop adventure courses at numerous locations around the country, typically featuring more than a hundred obstacles per park. Those obstacles include ziplines, swinging bridges, cargo nets and platform challenges, spread across courses ranging from around 2m to 25m above the ground and graded by difficulty so visitors can choose a level that suits their confidence and experience. This format tends to suit families and mixed-ability groups particularly well, since lower, easier courses sit alongside higher, more challenging ones at the same site, letting each person self-select their comfort level.

## Safety standards for challenge courses and zip lines

Well-run challenge courses and zip lines aren't ad hoc infrastructure: the current international benchmark is the ANSI/ACCT 03-2019 Challenge Courses and Canopy/Zip Line Tours Standard, published by ACCT International (the Association for Challenge Course Technology), which covers installation, operation and inspection requirements for this kind of aerial adventure infrastructure. In Australia, ACCT standards are used as a reference point for challenge-course element condition assessment alongside relevant Standards Australia work, giving operators (and visitors) a recognised benchmark for what "properly maintained and inspected" actually looks like. It's a reasonable question to ask a course operator directly if you're unsure: a well-run site should be able to speak to its inspection and maintenance regime.

## Who high ropes courses suit

High ropes courses and adventure parks are designed to be accessible to a much wider range of ages, fitness levels and prior experience than technical climbing, canyoning or even via ferrata: most self-guided courses use a full-body harness clipped into a continuous belay system, so you can't unclip and fall even if you lose your footing. That said, specific age, height and weight requirements do vary by operator and by course level, since the lowest, easiest courses typically suit younger children while the highest, hardest ones are pitched at confident older kids and adults. Always check the specific requirements for your chosen park and course before booking, particularly if you're planning a family visit. ## Frequently asked questions

### What's the difference between a high ropes course and via ferrata?

High ropes courses use a continuous belay system on a fixed, purpose-built structure (often through trees), suited to a very wide range of ages and abilities. Via ferrata is climbing along a real cliff face using fixed cables and rungs, generally more physically demanding and typically guided rather than self-guided.

### Do I need prior experience to try a high ropes course?

No. High ropes courses are designed for people with no prior climbing or rope experience, using continuous belay systems so you stay attached throughout, with courses graded by difficulty so you can choose a level that suits your confidence.

### What safety standards do Australian high ropes courses follow?

The current international benchmark is the ANSI/ACCT 03-2019 Challenge Courses and Canopy/Zip Line Tours Standard, published by ACCT International, used in Australia as a reference point for challenge-course element condition assessment alongside relevant Standards Australia work.

### Are there age or height restrictions for high ropes courses?

Yes, though specific requirements vary by operator and by course level within the same park. Lower, easier courses are typically suited to younger children, while higher, harder courses are pitched at confident older kids and adults. Check with your chosen operator before booking.

### Where is Australia's highest zipline?

Illawarra Fly Treetop Adventures, in the Illawarra rainforest near Jamberoo south of Sydney, is officially recognised as home to Australia's highest zipline, part of a site that also includes an elevated Treetop Walk and the Knights Tower lookout.

### Are high ropes courses suitable for people afraid of heights?

They can be a good, controlled way to build confidence with height, since you're continuously clipped into a belay system and courses are graded by difficulty. Starting on a lower, easier course before progressing to higher levels is a sensible approach if height genuinely worries you.
