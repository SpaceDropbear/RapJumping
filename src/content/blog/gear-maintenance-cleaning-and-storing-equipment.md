## TL;DR

Clean, inspect, and store your abseiling gear regularly to boost safety and lifespan.

- **Carabiners:** soak, scrub, rinse, lubricate, store dry.
- **Rope:** wash with mild soap or rope cleaner, air-dry in shade.
- **Harness:** spot-clean, air-dry, check for frays and damage.
- **Storage:** keep all gear dry, away from UV and chemicals.  
  Routine maintenance means safer climbs, smoother descents, and fewer costly replacements.

You know that moment when you're halfway through packing for your next abseiling adventure, only to realise your carabiners are a bit grimy and your rope is dusty? That's precisely why climbing gear care is so important. Whether you're new to abseiling or you've spent years on the rock, a little maintenance goes a long way toward keeping your equipment safe and boosting its lifespan. Plus, when your gear feels clean and ready, you'll climb (and descend) with far more confidence. Ready to get started? Let's walk through these simple steps together so you can keep all your essentials in top-notch condition.

If you're also looking to expand your kit, don't forget to check out our [abseiling gear](/complete-essential-abseiling-gear-checklist/). It's always good to see what new innovations might simplify your routine when you're heading out on your next trip.

## Prepare your cleaning station

Before you jump into any scrubbing or rinsing, set up a space where you can comfortably work without worrying about damaging indoor floors or your lovely coffee table. If you have a garage or well-ventilated area, that's ideal. If not, a spacious corner (with some plastic sheeting laid down) can also do the job.

### What you'll need

- A large bucket or tub for soaking.
- Warm water and a mild soap (or specialised rope cleaning solution).
- Soft brushes for scrubbing away dirt.
- Microfibre cloth or clean rags for drying.
- Lubricant (wax-based or Teflon-based) for your carabiner gates.
- Compressed air (optional) to blast away stubborn debris.

Gather everything first so you're not hunting around the house mid-clean. Once you have your supplies staged, you can move on to the fun part.

## Inspect each piece carefully

This is where your detective hat comes in. Inspecting your gear regularly might be the single best step you can take to extend its life. It's also a key safety measure. After all, abseiling gear that's compromised is no joke.

- Look for cracks, dents or sharp edges on metal parts.
- Check fabrics (like harnesses or slings) for fraying, tears or burns.
- Flex your rope gently and see if there are any flat spots, cuts or stiff sections.
- Tug on buckles or webbing to feel for weak spots.

If you spot major damage-like a cracked carabiner or a harness with structural wear-retire that item immediately. The same goes for a rope that's taken a heavy fall. Safety always comes first.

## Clean your carabiners

Carabiners are often the workhorses of your climbing system. They can last over 15 years if handled with care, though daily use in harsh conditions can shorten their lifespan to as little as 5 years. Regular cleaning keeps them performing well and helps you catch any hidden damage.

### Step 1: Soak and scrub

Place your carabiners in a bucket or sink filled with warm, soapy water. Gently swirl them around, then let them soak for a few minutes to loosen dirt. Use a soft brush to focus on the pivot points and inside the gate area. If you've noticed burrs or small sharp edges, a light sanding with fine-grit sandpaper can help smooth things out. Make sure you don't remove too much metal-just a gentle pass to eliminate rough spots.

### Step 2: Rinse away debris

Drain the soapy water, then refill your bucket with clean, warm water. Rinse the carabiners thoroughly to remove any leftover soap or gunk. If you still see stubborn particles, a quick spray of compressed air can help dislodge them. Double-check everything, especially the hinge mechanism, to ensure you're not leaving dirt behind.

### Step 3: Lubricate the gates

Once your carabiners are clean and dry, apply a small amount of lubricant to the gate, spring and rivet areas. Wax-based or Teflon-based lubes work well (just avoid anything oil-based that could attract more grime). Wipe off any excess so it doesn't drip onto your rope. Flip the gate a few times to spread the lubricant evenly.

If you need more tips on selecting the right type of carabiner for your adventures, check out [Carabiners: How to Choose](https://www.rei.com/learn/expert-advice/carabiners.html). It covers shapes, strength ratings and gate mechanisms.

## Wash your rope

A dirty rope doesn't just look grim, it can also wear out faster and be more prone to damage. If you notice your hands or belay gloves turning black after handling your rope, it's probably time for a wash.

### When to wash

Many climbers wait far too long before cleaning their ropes. Inspect your rope regularly, especially the first few metres where knots are tied and friction is highest. If you see dirt or your rope feels stiff, a gentle cleaning can help restore its flexibility. Definitely wash if your rope has been exposed to sand, grit or salty conditions.

### How to wash

You can hand-wash your rope in a bathtub or use a front-loading washing machine. When machine washing, it helps to “daisy chain” your rope: just double it, tie a slip knot, then keep pulling loops through loops until it's fully chained. This reduces tangling during the wash.

- Use warm water only, or a dedicated rope-cleaning product like Beal Rope Cleaner or Sterling Wicked Good Rope Wash.
- Avoid using standard detergents or bleach, which can weaken fibres.
- Run a gentle cycle with slow spin speed.
- Once washed, lay the rope out to air-dry in a cool, shaded spot.

Make sure your rope is fully dry before packing it away. A damp rope is more prone to mildew, and you'd be surprised how long it can stay wet if you coil it up too soon.

## Look after your harness

Your harness is one piece of gear that's definitely close to your heart (and essential for, well, keeping you alive). It's designed to handle the stress of catches and falls, but it can degrade over time.

### Check for wear

Give your harness a quick, routine once-over before every outing. That means examining your belay loop, waist belt and leg loops for signs of fraying or unusual thinning. Although harnesses can last up to seven years or so, heavy use, frequent falls and exposure to sun can shorten that drastically. If you find tears, heavy abrasion or any structural damage, it's time to say goodbye to that harness.

### Clean gently

A harness generally needs just a quick spot-clean with warm water and mild soap. If it's very dirty, soak it in lukewarm water for a few minutes, then scrub gently with a soft brush. Rinse thoroughly and hang it to air-dry in a well-ventilated space. Don't toss it in a dryer or put it near a heater, as intense heat can damage the webbing.

## Store everything properly

Once all your equipment is clean, the final step is to store it somewhere that won't undo your hard work. Proper storage is just as critical as proper cleaning. It keeps your gear safe from moisture, dust and harsh UV rays.

### Carabiner storage tips

Carabiners can corrode if left in a damp environment for too long. To keep them pristine:

- Store them in a sealed container or bag to minimise humidity.
- Avoid extreme temperature swings, such as leaving them in a sweltering car boot or a freezing shed.
- Keep them away from corrosive materials or chemicals.

A simple plastic bin with a lid often does the trick if you're storing them long-term. If you're frequently on the move, a mesh bag might be more convenient so your carabiners can breathe.

### Rope storage tips

Using a rope bag is a great way to keep your rope clean and tangle-free. It also helps you transport your rope more easily.

- Coil or daisy-chain your rope loosely so it doesn't develop kinks or permanent bends.
- Keep it in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight.
- Double-check the dryness before you store it-damp rope in a dark, sealed environment can become mouldy.

If you spot any new abrasions or suspect your rope has taken a hefty fall, log the incident to keep track. A detailed log (including date of purchase, falls and use frequency) makes it simpler to decide when to retire your rope.

### Harness storage tips

Harnesses don't demand anything fancy when it comes to storage, but they do benefit from a consistent environment.

- Hang your harness in a closet or place it in a gear bin.
- Make sure it's fully dry to prevent mildew.
- Avoid direct sunlight in long-term storage, since UV exposure can weaken webbing over time.

If you ever notice major wear when pulling it out for your next trip, don't risk it. Replace any harness that's questionable.

### FAQs

**Q: How often should I clean my climbing rope?**  
A: Wash when it feels stiff, looks dirty, or after exposure to sand, grit, or salt. Regular light cleaning can extend its lifespan.

**Q: Can I use normal detergent to clean climbing gear?**  
A: No-use mild soap or purpose-made rope/harness cleaners to avoid damaging fibres and coatings.

**Q: How long can I use my harness before replacing it?**  
A: With light use, up to 7 years; heavy use or visible wear means replacing much sooner. Always retire it if you find structural damage.

**Q: What's the best way to store carabiners?**  
A: Keep them clean, dry, and away from corrosive materials or extreme temperatures-ideally in a gear bag or bin.

**Q: Do I need to lubricate carabiners?**  
A: Yes-apply a wax- or Teflon-based lubricant to gate and hinge points after cleaning to keep them smooth and free of grit.
