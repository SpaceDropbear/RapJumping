## What Carnarvon Gorge actually is

Carnarvon Gorge sits within Carnarvon National Park, in Central Queensland's Sandstone Belt, and is built around a network of fully signposted walking trails leading from the main access road and visitor centre into the gorge itself. It's a genuinely spectacular landscape: towering sandstone cliffs, permanent spring-fed pools and side gorges, significant Aboriginal rock art sites, and lush vegetation that stands out dramatically against the surrounding dry sandstone country. The park's own material describes it around walking, exploring side gorges and visiting Aboriginal art sites, with no mention of canyoning-specific infrastructure like fixed abseil anchors or guided canyon-descent routes.

## Why it's not a canyoning destination

The straightforward reason is that Carnarvon Gorge has never been developed or managed as a canyoning site. Unlike Behana Gorge in North Queensland (which has guided tours built specifically around abseiling, natural rock slides and swimming), Carnarvon Gorge's infrastructure is entirely walking-trail based, and Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service's own visitor material makes no reference to canyoning, abseiling or technical gorge-descent activities anywhere in the park. If the dramatic cliffs and side gorges have you picturing a canyoning trip, that instinct isn't wrong about the scenery: it's just pointed at the wrong activity for this particular park.

## What Carnarvon Gorge is genuinely worth visiting for

None of this makes Carnarvon Gorge a lesser destination: it's simply a different one, and a genuinely excellent hiking destination in its own right. The main gorge trail leads to a series of notable side gorges and features, and a minimum of three days is recommended to properly explore the main track, the side gorges and the Aboriginal rock art sites rather than rushing through in a single day. The park's remoteness and lack of technical infrastructure are part of the appeal for hikers specifically seeking a quieter, walking-focused experience rather than a crowded, activity-packed one.

## Permits and planning a visit

No permit is required for a day visit to Carnarvon Gorge. If you're planning to camp overnight or walk the multi-day Carnarvon Great Walk, however, a permit is required and fees apply: both are booked through Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service, either online or via the Emerald Visitor Information Centre. Given the park's remoteness, rangers recommend contacting them at least 10 days ahead of a planned walk to check current trail and weather conditions, and booking camping or Great Walk permits at least six weeks in advance given demand for this popular park.

## Where to actually go canyoning in Queensland instead

If technical canyoning (abseiling, rock slides and swimming through a gorge) is genuinely what you're after, Carnarvon Gorge isn't the destination. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can you go canyoning at Carnarvon Gorge?

No. Carnarvon Gorge is managed around a network of walking trails, not technical canyoning infrastructure. Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service's own visitor material makes no reference to abseiling or guided canyon-descent activities in this park.

### What is Carnarvon Gorge actually known for?

Hiking and walking: sandstone cliffs, spring-fed side gorges, significant Aboriginal rock art sites, and lush vegetation contrasting with the surrounding dry sandstone country, all accessed via a network of fully signposted trails.

### Do I need a permit to visit Carnarvon Gorge?

Not for a day visit. A permit is required for overnight camping and for the multi-day Carnarvon Great Walk, both booked through Queensland Parks and Wildlife Service or the Emerald Visitor Information Centre.

### How many days should I spend at Carnarvon Gorge?

A minimum of three days is recommended to properly explore the main gorge trail, its side gorges and the Aboriginal art sites, rather than rushing through in a single day.

### Where can I actually go canyoning in Queensland?

Behana Gorge near Cairns is Queensland's established guided-canyoning destination, and Cedar Creek Falls in the Gold Coast hinterland is a self-guided swimming-hole walk rather than a technical canyon. Both are covered in our dedicated Queensland canyoning guide.

### How far ahead should I book a Carnarvon Gorge trip?

Rangers recommend contacting them at least 10 days ahead of a planned walk to confirm current conditions, and booking camping or Carnarvon Great Walk permits at least six weeks in advance given demand.
