## **The Global Standard: IRATA International & IRATA Australia**

When it comes to industrial rope access, safety, and best practices are paramount. The **Industrial Rope Access Trade Association (IRATA International)** is the only truly global trade association in the work-at-height sector, setting the gold standard for training and operational safety worldwide. In Australia, **IRATA Australia** serves as the peak industry association, upholding these rigorous international benchmarks.

IRATA Australia represents the interests of over 30 member companies, including prominent names in the field, and collectively employs more than 500 rope access technicians across the nation. These member companies are incredibly diverse, operating across a wide range of sectors such as commercial property, engineering, construction, mining and gas, and telecommunications. Beyond representation, IRATA Australia plays a vital role in developing the workforce, training over 2,000 new rope access technicians annually and servicing more than 5,000 client companies nationally. This robust framework ensures consistency and excellence in an industry built on precision and safety.

## **Qualifications: Becoming an Industrial Rope Access Technician**

To embark on a career as an industrial rope access technician, you must complete formal training and obtain a recognised technical grade. **IRATA International** operates a comprehensive training, assessment, and certification scheme that is applied uniformly to all its members globally. Based on their experience and the level of training and assessment completed, rope access technicians are grouped into one of three technical grades:

- **Level 1:** A Level 1 rope access technician is capable of performing a specified range of tasks under the direct supervision of a Level 3 technician. This is your foundational entry point, teaching you the basics of rope systems, personal protective equipment, and fundamental manoeuvres.
- **Level 2:** Building on Level 1 skills, Level 2 technicians possess more complex rigging, rescue, and advanced rope access skills. They can assist in more intricate operations and work under the supervision of a Level 3 technician, taking on greater responsibility.
- **Level 3:** The highest level, Level 3 technicians are proficient in all skills applicable to Level 1 and 2. Crucially, they are deemed capable of taking complete responsibility for rope access safety in relation to work projects. This senior role requires a deep understanding of relevant work techniques and legislation, extensive knowledge of advanced rigging and rescue procedures, a current first aid certificate, and comprehensive knowledge of the IRATA training, assessment, and certification scheme itself.

Training and certification can be acquired via IRATA Australia member companies, all of whom operate strictly in line with the IRATA training and certification scheme. Every course assessment is conducted by independent IRATA personnel, ensuring impartiality and adherence to global standards.

## **What Sort of Jobs Can Rope Access Technicians Do?**

Rope access technicians are required for an incredibly broad range of jobs - virtually any task that involves a difficult-to-access area at height or in confined spaces where traditional scaffolding or elevated work platforms are impractical or too costly.

Technicians find themselves undertaking diverse roles, including:

- **Façade Maintenance:** Critical building repair, maintenance, and cleaning for high-rise structures. This often includes specialised window cleaning.
- **Non-Destructive Testing (NDT):** Inspecting structures, pipelines, and equipment for flaws without causing damage, often in challenging industrial environments.
- **Confined Space Work:** Safely operating within restricted areas like boilers, cooling towers, shafts, and water tanks for inspection, cleaning, or repair.
- **Geotechnical Work:** Essential for infrastructure projects, involving tasks like rock demolition, rock fall netting installation, and soil stabilisation on cliffsides.
- **Rigging Services & Displays:** Installing promotional, marketing, and seasonal displays in large shopping centres, public spaces, or at events.
- **Oil & Gas Industry:** Conducting maintenance, inspection, and repair work on offshore oil rigs and onshore processing plants.
- **Renewable Energy:** Playing a vital role in the maintenance and repairs of wind turbines and other renewable energy infrastructure.

These varied roles offer a dynamic and challenging career path for those who thrive on practical problem-solving at height.

## **How Safe Are These Jobs?**

When you see some of the precarious spaces rope access technicians are required to access, safety certainly comes to mind. Historically, working at heights was a far more dangerous occupation. Consider the "High Scalers" who built the Hoover Dam in the 1930s; they worked using primitive Bosun's Chairs (a simple wooden plank seat at the end of a rope). During the dam's construction, 93 such workers tragically lost their lives, demonstrating just how risky this kind of work was in the past.

Today, industrial rope access technicians enjoy an excellent safety record, one of the best in the work-at-height sector. This remarkable improvement is due to a highly regulated industry, a widespread and stringent training and accreditation scheme (like IRATA's), and the mandatory use of state-of-the-art equipment. These factors mean that working as a rope access technician in countries like Australia is now considered very safe, with incidents being extremely rare.
