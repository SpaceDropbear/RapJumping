If you suffer from acrophobia, you're certainly not alone - a significant number of people are intensely scared of heights!

According to the British Homeopathic Association, the term acrophobia is derived from the Greek words ‘akron' (meaning heights) and ‘phobos' (meaning fear). A 2009 article published in the *Journal of Anxiety Disorders* estimates that around one in twenty adults suffer from acrophobia. It's an irrational fear where, for some people, standing on the edge of a high balcony elicits a profound physiological response, while others can feel the effects of acrophobia just by standing on a chair. In his book, *Why Civilizations Self-Destruct*, American sociologist E. Pendell describes acrophobia as ‘inherited and derived from mutations in the hundreds of millions of years of our evolutionary span'. Simply put, this phobia is more than likely an evolutionary survival mechanism - a deep-seated instinct to avoid situations where we could fall and become injured.

Whether your fear of heights is debilitating or relatively mild, it is often possible to overcome it. If you want to try your hand at **rappelling** or **abseiling**, or even experience the unique thrill of **Rap Jumping**, it pays to have a plan of attack in place so you're able to conquer your fear of heights on the day. While we always recommend seeing a qualified psychologist if your phobia is severe, the following tips may help to ease those pre-jump butterflies and guide you towards your next great **adventure**!

## **1. Take it Slow: Embrace Desensitization**

If you have a fairly severe fear of heights, it's important to tackle your fear one careful step at a time. While once upon a time psychologists believed in literally throwing patients 'in the deep end,' they now recognise that it's often best to overcome your fears at your own pace - gradual desensitization is the key.

If you're considering an experience like **Rap Jumping** or **abseiling**, try taking a couple of smaller steps first - long before you take the actual leap! Visit rooftop balconies, observation decks, and other places where you can (safely) look over the edge and observe how you feel. If fear begins to flood your senses, utilise the techniques mentioned in tip two (below). The idea is to keep placing yourself in these situations until you feel more at ease, slowly expanding your comfort zone.

## **2. Take a Moment: Master Your Physical Reaction**

If you're afraid of heights, you're probably well aware of that feeling of panic that sets in the moment you look over the edge! This feeling of panic is very physical; it's often characterised by an increased heart rate, butterflies in your stomach, dizziness, light-headedness, and sweaty palms, among other things.

The trick here is to learn how to control this physical reaction you experience when at heights. Before you look over the edge or step into a challenging situation, take a moment. Centre yourself, take a deep, slow breath, and focus your mind on a single point or a calming thought. Engage in mindful breathing or a quick mental scan of your body to ground yourself. This can significantly help to calm your nerves and reduce the intensity of your physiological response, allowing you to approach the situation with more control.

## **3. Question Why You Are Afraid: Trust in Safety**

Generally, people are scared of heights because they fear falling and getting hurt. When considering an **extreme sport** like **Rap Jumping™** or **rappelling**, it's natural to question the risks, especially if fear of injury is a concern. It's important to remember here that while some **extreme sports** do carry a higher inherent injury risk, professional **abseiling** and **rap jumping** experiences are designed with paramount safety in mind.

Reputable providers of these **adrenaline**-fueled activities, which you can find through Rap Jumping, prioritise your well-being above all else. They employ highly qualified and extensively trained instructors who know the ropes better than anyone. They use high-quality, regularly inspected, and certified equipment, including secure harnesses, and adhere to strict safety protocols. Many of these commercial operations boast long-standing records of incident-free operations, a testament to their unwavering commitment to participant safety. So, if your fear stems from a concern about getting hurt, remember that professional **rap jumping** (or **abseiling**/**rappelling**) experiences are regulated and specifically engineered for safety.

## **4. You Are in Control: Embrace Personal Agency**

Unlike some **adventure sports** that involve free-falling or being completely passive, **Rap Jumping™** uniquely places you, the participant, in the driver's seat. This distinctive style of **rappelling** allows you to control your speed of descent - from the second you leave the edge of the building to the moment your feet touch the ground. You're not just falling; you're actively 'running' down the wall, dictating your pace.

This aspect of personal control is a significant source of comfort for many, especially those grappling with acrophobia. If you don't want to fly down the side of the building at top speed, you absolutely don't have to! Furthermore, reputable providers offering **rap jumping** experiences always have a trained brake person present for every jump as an added safety measure. This means that if you find yourself struggling to complete your descent, they are able to slowly and safely lower you down to the ground, ensuring your comfort and security throughout the entire process.

## **5. Keep in Mind How Great You'll Feel at the End: The Power of Achievement**

Overcoming your fears is an incredibly rewarding experience, and conquering acrophobia is no exception. If you suffer from a **fear of heights**, you'll be amazed at how relieved, accomplished, and utterly exhilarated you will feel once your feet hit the ground after an **abseiling** or **Rap Jumping™** experience.

Completing an activity such as **rap jumping** can have a profound impact, helping to alleviate your fear of heights in day-to-day life; it's a real, tangible achievement that builds immense confidence. Overcoming your **fear of heights** can be incredibly liberating, opening up new possibilities for **adventure** and personal growth. Many participants, even those initially most scared, often find themselves wanting to do it all again, empowered by their newfound courage!
