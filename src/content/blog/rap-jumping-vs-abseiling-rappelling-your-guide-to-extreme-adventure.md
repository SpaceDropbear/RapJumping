## TL;DR

Rap Jumping is an extreme rope descent sport that combines elements of several techniques.

- **Abseiling (Standard Rappel):** Backwards descent facing the wall, steady and controlled - good for beginners.
- **Aussie Rappelling:** Forward-facing descent developed by the Australian SAS in the 1960s, higher adrenaline, brake hand in front.
- **Deepelling:** Canadian version of Aussie rappel, brake hand positioned behind the descender, rare to find.
- **Angel Jumping:** Forward-facing freefall from a slack rope before braking, huge adrenaline hit.
- **Rap Jumping (Original Method):** A mix of Aussie rappel and angel jumping, allowing forward-facing wall jumps with both self-control and a brake person for safety.

Main takeaway: These rope sports may look similar, but each has its own style, technique, and adrenaline level. Rap Jumping was designed to be the most dynamic and thrilling of them all.

### Understanding Abseiling (Standard Rappel)

**Abseiling**, also widely known as the ‘standard rappel,' is defined as the controlled descent of a vertical drop by way of a fixed rope. During **abseiling**, your feet remain in contact with the vertical surface, and the participant essentially walks backwards down the wall while letting the rope slide steadily through a belay device.

The descent in **abseiling** is performed backwards - meaning the participant faces the vertical surface. This is the key difference when comparing standard **abseiling** with other descent methods like Aussie **rappelling**, angel jumping, and deepelling. It's an excellent way to get started with rope descent and build confidence, making it a perfect introduction to **adventure sports**.

---

### Australian Rappelling (Aussie Rappel)

Australian **rappelling** - or the “Aussie rappel” as it's more commonly referred to - is a forward-facing SRT (Single Rope Technique) rappel developed by the Australian SAS in the 1960s. Think forward-facing **abseiling** if you're still confused! This method significantly increases the **adrenaline** rush.

The participant is attached to the rope via a ‘descender' and ‘carabiner,' which is typically attached to the rear of the harness. The participant descends the vertical face facing down, keeping their hands at the front of their body for braking. Aussie **rappelling** is a dynamic and visually impressive form of descent.

---

### Deepelling: The Canadian Connection

Deepelling is essentially the Canadian version of the Aussie Rappel, derived from the Australian method. As with Aussie Rappel, participants descend facing forward. However, a key distinction is that their rope-side hand is positioned behind the descender (often a figure-of-eight device).

According to an article published in The Globe and Mail, deepelling is a rare **adventure sport**, offered in only a couple of places: Grand Falls, New Brunswick, and at a city tower in Décalade, Quebec. Its limited availability makes it a unique **extreme sports** experience.

---

### Angel Jumping: The Freefall Element

It's quite common for the term ‘angel jump' to be thrown into the mix when talking about **Rap Jumping**.

Also known as ‘rope rush,' ‘angel diving,' and ‘slack rope Australian **rappelling**,' this method generally requires a large overhanging pitch. The participant pulls a certain amount of slack rope through the descender before jumping, attaching it to the front of the harness using a carabiner. The participant then jumps from the edge and freefalls until the rope tightens and the descender takes up the slack. The participant either stops themselves, or keeps their hands off the rope and allows a brake person to stop them. When braking themselves, the participant keeps one hand by their side and the other behind their back (as per standard **abseiling**). This method offers an incredible burst of **adrenaline**!

### Understanding Rap Jumping - A Historical Overview

The original **Rap Jumping** method, on which our company was founded, was a commercialized version of the Aussie rappel, essentially a cross between Aussie rappel and angel jumping, designed to deliver an unparalleled **adrenaline** experience.

In this unique method, participants descended facing forward and were allowed to ‘jump' away from the wall, creating a truly dynamic and exhilarating experience. Safety was always the number one priority, with participants maintaining control of their descent speed while a dedicated brake person provided assistance. While this specific activity is no longer offered by Rap Jumping, understanding its unique characteristics provides valuable insight into the evolution of **extreme sports** and rope **adventure**.
