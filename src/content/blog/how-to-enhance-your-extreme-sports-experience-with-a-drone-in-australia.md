## 1. Use Drones to Scout and Preview the Event

Every smart extreme sports setup begins with recon. In the past, this meant walking the site with a camera or relying on grainy headcam footage. It was essential, sure - but boring to watch. Now with a **drone fly-through**, you can turn recon into a **visual teaser** that builds hype before the event.

Imagine flying your drone:

- Down a vertical cliff face you're about to jump,
- Through the tight urban corridors you'll be descending during a building run,
- Or over a rugged mountain bike trail you're preparing to dominate.

Not only does it help you plan the route and assess risks, but it also doubles as a **promo video** to get fans excited, boost ticket sales, or encourage online engagement. Post your recon footage on platforms like **Instagram Reels, TikTok, or YouTube Shorts** to build buzz in the lead-up.

**Tip for Aussies:** Use your drone footage to enter film competitions like the *Australian Drone Film Festival* or to pitch local sponsors who want fresh, adventurous content.

## 2. Capture Next-Level Footage During the Event

Helmet cams and handheld cameras still have their place, but they can't match the **stability, scope, and movement** that drones offer. Especially in fast-paced, large-scale environments, drones give you that **"God's eye view"** that turns a backyard stunt into something that looks Hollywood-made.

Here's what you can do:

- **Follow the action** from above with smooth tracking shots.
- **Zoom over crowds**, ramps, and jumps.
- **Capture dynamic transitions** from start lines to finish zones.

With good planning, your drone operator can choreograph movements to follow key athletes or jumpers throughout the event. Combine that with ground footage later for a **multi-angle highlight reel**.

**Drone filming during Rap Jumping™** has shown us just how epic a controlled descent down a skyscraper can look when viewed from above, below, and even side-on at mid-levels.

**Aussie-friendly drones:** Look into the **DJI Air 3** or **DJI Mini 4 Pro** - they strike a solid balance between camera quality and portability, and they're compliant with **CASA drone regulations**.

## Australian Drone Laws for Extreme Sports Filming

Before you send your drone flying off a cliff, make sure you understand **Australian drone regulations**, especially from the **Civil Aviation Safety Authority (CASA)**.

Quick checklist:

- Keep your drone **within visual line of sight (VLOS)**.
- Fly **below 120 metres** (400 feet).
- Stay at least **30 metres from people** not involved in the activity.
- Avoid flying near **emergency zones or airports**.
- Register drones **over 250g**, and get a **flyer accreditation** for commercial use.

For bigger events or more complex shoots, consider working with a **licensed drone operator** who understands the legal landscape and can help you get the perfect shot **without risking a CASA fine**.
